Practicing and keeping it

Knowing an alg isn't the same as using it in a solve. Here's what to practise, how to do it step by step, and how to keep your cases without relearning them.

The four things to train

In a solve, ZBLL is three quick steps: recognise the case, do the alg, do the last U turn. Practice should cover each part:

1. Recognition speed

Seeing which case it is, quickly and reliably.

2. Alg speed

Fast, smooth turning, with the alg in your muscle memory.

3. Case → alg connection

Seeing the case and knowing its alg straight away, not just knowing the alg on its own.

4. The final AUF

Knowing which way to turn U at the end, from one sticker you check, instead of looking around.

Step by step

  1. Learn each alg on its own with good turning. To link it to its case, do the alg backwards (its inverse) from a solved cube and look at the case it makes. In ZBTrain, click a case for its setup and press Play to watch the alg.
  2. Pick an AUF sticker for each alg: one sticker that tells you the final U turn without searching. ZBTrain shows every alg with its pre- and post-AUF for the scramble you got.
  3. Drill each alg until it's under about 1.5 seconds. Over time, aim for 1–1.2 seconds.
  4. Practise them mixed. Select the group in ZBTrain and use Recap, which gives each case once. Do a few recaps focusing on clean recognition, turning and your AUF sticker.
  5. Then go for speed: recaps where recognition plus execution has to beat a target time.
  6. Use them in real solves as soon as you can.
💡

Don't practise all 12 at once on the first day. Learn them in halves (6) or thirds (4), so each case comes up more often while it's new.

Speed goals

  • Per alg: under 1.5 seconds to start with, 1–1.2 seconds eventually.
  • Recognition + execution together: under 3 seconds is a good first target. Faster solvers aim for low 2s or under 2.
  • Or relative to your solves: the last layer usually takes about a third of your solve, so aim for a third of your average.

Keeping it: spaced repetition

Algs you don't revisit fade. The fix is spaced repetition: review a group after a day or two, then after a few days, then weekly, with each review a little further apart. Every time you remember a case, it takes longer to forget it.

A review doesn't mean relearning: go through each case a few times and do a recap in the trainer. That takes about 5–7 minutes, and it's a good warm-up before a session. Doing solves also helps, since with ZBLS you get a ZBLL case every solve.

ZBTrain's Learn page does the scheduling for you:

  • Each day you get your new cases plus the ones due for review.
  • After each solve, the trainer suggests how well you knew the case (from mistakes, pauses and recognition time), and you can change it.
  • Cases you know well come back less and less often. Ones you miss come back sooner.

Useful tools in ZBTrain

  • Virtual cube: practise from the keyboard with no scrambling, for lots of reps.
  • Smart cube: practise on a real cube with your moves recorded.
  • Alg detection and trouble spots: see which alg you actually used, and where in an alg you usually pause or make mistakes.
  • Slow cases: drill just the cases above a time you choose.
  • Weighted case order: slower cases come up more often.
