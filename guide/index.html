New to ZB? Start here

The ZB method finishes the cube with one algorithm for the whole last layer. It's famous for its huge alg count, but nobody learns 472 algs at once: you learn 12 at a time, in a sensible order, and keep them with short reviews. This guide shows you how.

What the ZB method is

ZB is CFOP with a different ending. You solve the cross and the first three F2L pairs as usual. Then, instead of inserting the last pair and doing OLL and PLL, you do two steps:

  1. ZBLS (ZB last slot): insert the last F2L pair and orient the last-layer edges, so all four show the top colour.
  2. ZBLL (ZB last layer): solve the entire last layer with one algorithm.

Once the edges are oriented, every possible last layer is a ZBLL case. That's one look and one alg, where OLL + PLL needs two looks and two algs.

Compared with finishing in two steps (OCLL then PLL), ZBLL saves around 8 moves on average, which is why top solvers in both ZZ and CFOP use it.

Is it worth learning?

Why people learn it

  • One look and one alg for the last layer
  • Fewer moves than OLL + PLL
  • Lots of short, fast algs (many use only R and U)
  • Useful from day one: any case you don't know yet, you still solve with OCLL + PLL

What makes it hard

  • 493 cases in total, 472 once you know PLL
  • Recognition takes practice: you read the corners and the edges
  • It needs oriented edges, so in plain CFOP it only comes up about 1 solve in 8 unless you also learn ZBLS
💡

The hardest part of ZB isn't any single alg: it's turning up every day. Learning a few cases at a time and reviewing them on a schedule (spaced repetition) is what gets people to the end. That's what ZBTrain's Learn page is built around.

How long it takes

ZBLL is grouped into sets of 12 cases that share a corner pattern. A common pace is one group of 12 a week: a few days to learn the algs, the rest of the week to drill them. At that pace, all 472 cases take about 40 weeks, roughly ten months.

That sounds long, but the time passes anyway, and each group pays off as soon as you know it. Keeping what you've learned is cheap: a few minutes of review a day, or a quick warm-up before a session.

A short history

  • 1981: Lars Petrus's method orients the last-layer edges early, which opens the door to solving the last layer in one step. The algs didn't exist yet.
  • 1980s: Treep and K. and H. Dockhorn generate solutions for every last layer (1,212 cases once symmetries and inverses are counted together).
  • 2000–2001: Bernard Helmstetter generates one-step last-layer algs, including the set with all edges oriented.
  • 2001: Zbigniew Zborowski and Ron van Bruchem independently add edge orientation to CFOP. That's where the "ZB" comes from, and they credited Helmstetter's algs on their sites.
  • 2005: the earliest known use of the name "ZBLL".
  • 2022: James Straughan proposes the names PHLL or HPLL, to credit Petrus and Helmstetter.
